🏗️ Ford City redevelopment clears Plan Commission — heads to City Council zoning committee

The Chicago Plan Commission voted Thursday (Aug. 20) to approve Kurv Industrial's ~$200M plan to demolish the shuttered Ford City Mall and replace it with a four-building, 45-foot industrial campus spanning ~1.09M SF (904 parking, 216 loading spaces, 160 truck stalls). The roll call was 6 yes, 6 abstain, 1 recusal and 1 no. The proposal now moves to the City Council's Zoning Committee — expected no later than October — then a full Council vote. The developer anticipates closing by end of 2026 with a ~2-year construction timeline, and projects ~185 permanent and ~90 construction jobs.

The proposal now heads to the City Council's zoning committee, expected no later than October, then a full Council vote. Developer counsel said closing is anticipated by end of year, with a ~2-year construction timeline (near end of 2028).

2. Plan Commission vote tally: 6 yes, 6 abstain, 1 recusal, 1 no on Ford City rezoning (Patch)

Patch published the full roll-call: DPD Commissioner Ciere Boatright, CDOT's William Cheeks and four others voted yes; six members (including chair Laura Flores) abstained citing emotional ties to the mall; Ald. Derrick Curtis (18th) recused; Ald. Byron Sigcho-Lopez (25th) cast the lone no vote. Several commissioners questioned whether Kurv's proposal received enough community scrutiny.

3. Kurv Industrial plans $200M redevelopment of Ford City Mall near Midway (Chicago Business Journal)

Kurv Industrial plans to transform the shuttered indoor mall into warehouses as part of a ~$200 million project near Chicago Midway International Airport. The redevelopment — four 45-ft industrial buildings totaling ~1.09M SF with 904 parking, 216 loading spaces and 160 truck stalls — is projected to create about 185 permanent jobs and 90 construction jobs.

Links Chicago Business Journal • ABC7 (renderings, jobs)
4. Neighbors object as Ford City industrial campus advances: 'warehouses are at the bottom' (CBS News)

The Plan Commission approved the industrial conversion despite objections from West Lawn neighbors, who raised concerns over truck traffic, air and noise pollution, and the loss of potential retail/commercial development. Protesters rallied outside City Hall ahead of the vote; Ald. Sigcho-Lopez pushed back, saying of economic development options, 'I do believe that warehouses are at the bottom.'

Links CBS (protests + approval) • FOX 32
5. Moving Archer Forward corridor plan set for adoption at Aug. 20 Plan Commission (Chicago Construction News)

Alongside Ford City, the Aug. 20 Plan Commission agenda carried a resolution adopting the Moving Archer Forward corridor plan — a DPD + RTA planning effort for the 6.5-mile Archer Avenue corridor (Halsted to Cicero) spanning Archer Heights, Bridgeport, Brighton Park, Garfield Ridge and McKinley Park. The plan projects ~800 housing units, 280,000 SF of retail and ~835 jobs over 10-20 years across 31 ETOD sites.
